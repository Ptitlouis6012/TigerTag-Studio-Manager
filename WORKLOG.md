# Worklog — v2.21.2 (in progress)

## Added
- A widget is **bound to its machine from the start**, so it travels with it without anyone having to group them: binding by hand the first time would make the obvious case the one that needs work, and a widget left behind by a machine that moved is the failure everyone notices. It is a real binding — releasable like any other, and the outline, the group move and the right-click menu already know what to do with it — `renderer/inventory.js`
- A widget switched on takes the **first free place** going down its machine's column: straight under the card when that is clear — where a new widget belongs, right against the thing it describes — and otherwise past whatever is in the way, into the first gap that fits. Walking down rather than jumping to the bottom means a widget switched off and on again returns to the hole it left instead of queueing behind the ones below it — so a machine's belongings read as a column that belongs to it rather than as pieces scattered near it. Always below, even if something else is sitting there: the board's own rule already covers that — overlapping is allowed and drawn in red rather than prevented — and a predictable place you may need to tidy beats an unpredictable one you have to hunt for. This is also what fixed the temperature widget appearing anywhere: which used to appear at the bottom of the board where nobody would find it: the placement worked out a widget's owner from its id prefix, and that ladder only listed the kinds that existed when it was written. It asks the board instead, so every kind of widget past and future is covered. rather than at the end of a scan that starts underneath: candidates are laid on a grid of card-sized cells centred on the machine and tried in order of distance, so beside beats under and under beats two cards away, whichever side happens to be free. Splitting and grouping keep their own anchor — those happen where the user did them — `renderer/inventory.js`
- Switching a widget off forgets where it was, so switching it back on brings it beside its machine rather than to wherever it last stood — which, after any arranging, is somewhere the user is not looking and possibly off the visible board. Only the widget you deliberately hid loses its place — `renderer/inventory.js`
- Each machine's widgets are switched on or off from its ⋮, as toggles — label left, switch right, the way a settings row reads everywhere else. A tick says "this happened"; a switch says "this is a thing you set", which is what these are. Flipping one leaves the menu open — turning two widgets on should not mean opening the menu twice. Four separate things had to stop closing it, and the last was the real one: writing the setting comes back as a Firestore snapshot, and that snapshot re-renders the whole view — so the menu shut a moment after the click, by a route that had nothing to do with the click. An open menu now DEFERS the rebuild, the way a card held in the hand already does — remembering it and reopening it afterwards did keep it on screen, but the seam showed: it blinked shut and back. Nothing is torn down at all now, and the debt is paid the moment the menu closes. The other three: the action runner, which closes for every other item because those take you somewhere; the full re-render, which took down the very menu the switch lives in; and the dismiss-on-scroll guard, since re-laying the board changes its size and that fires `scroll`. That last one is judged by POSITION, not by timing: a first attempt cleared a flag on a zero-delay timer, and the scroll events a resize provokes are delivered after that — queued for a later frame, not raised inline. A scroll whose offsets have not changed is the board resizing under its own contents, and both dismissal watchers now ignore it — the switches live on the machine's card, because a widget you have hidden has no menu of its own left to bring it back with. Absent means shown, so only a deliberate choice is ever written — `renderer/inventory.js`, `renderer/css/30-racks.css`
- **A temperature widget on the printer board** — the machine's own temperature pills, placed like anything else, with its own position (`tempPlan`). It renders each brand's existing temperature card rather than a seventh version of it, so the board and the panel cannot drift apart; read-only there, since the setpoint editors belong to the panel. It stays on the board when the machine is not talking, dimmed with an em dash for each reading — a widget that vanished when a printer slept would rearrange the dashboard around it, and the layout is the user's, not the app's. Only the numbers are swapped on a live tick, never the frame, so an open ⋮ survives a reading. It is the first board object that is not storage, and it needed no new board machinery — `renderer/inventory.js`, `renderer/printers/*/index.js`, `renderer/css/40-printers.css`
- **Cards can be bound together on the printer board, for good.** Pick a few — sweep them, or shift-click — then right-click and group them: from that moment, touching any one of them takes the whole set, so dragging one moves them all at their existing gaps, the way grouped objects behave in a drawing program. A dashed outline rings what belongs together, follows the set while it moves, and disappears the moment you ungroup — which leaves every card exactly where it stands. An ordinary selection stays what it was: transient, gone when you click away. **The binding lives on the members**, beside the coordinates it binds — `planCluster` on the machine's document for its card, `unitsPlanCluster` for its units widget, `units.{id}.planPrintersCluster` for a single unit — rather than in a collection of its own: no new security-rules block, deleting a machine takes its membership with it so nothing can point at a card that is gone, and a machine that is off keeps its binding for when it comes back. Called a *cluster* in the code because "group" already means three other things in this app, one of them ten lines away. The board also learned its first right-click menu, built out of the kebab menu's own component so the two cannot drift apart — `renderer/inventory.js`, `renderer/css/40-printers.css`, `renderer/css/70-detail-misc.css`, `assets/svg/icons/icon_unlink.svg`, `docs/firestore-schema.md`, `renderer/CODEMAP.md`
- **A connected machine's filament storage is shown as its UNITS** — one named block per physical object (Ext., AMS 1, AMS 2…) in the widget beside its card, rather than one flat strip, shown from the record at startup — dimmed and desaturated until the machine's link comes up, so last-known contents can never be mistaken for a live reading — and **Split units** in the widget's ⋮ turns each into its own object on the board, placeable where that box really stands. Grouped is the default because that is how most benches look, and the two arrangements are stored apart — the group's position on the machine, each unit's on the unit — so flipping between them never discards the other: an X1C with two AMS and a spool arm is three boxes standing in three places, and thirteen squares in a line said none of that. **The storage itself is stored on the machine**, as a `units` map on its own document — one entry per physical unit: an AMS, CFS or ACE box, a holder pair, the external arm. A map rather than an array so one unit can be written without rewriting its neighbours, and on the document rather than in a subcollection because the units are few, are always read with the machine, and would otherwise be invisible to anyone reading the printer document. Each carries its shape, a user-renamable label, a position on *each* board, and its slots: what the user assigned (`uids`) and what the machine last reported, stamped with `seenAt` so a reader knows how old it is. Every brand now describes its units, not just a flat slot list, from its own `PROTOCOL.md`. The seeder never overwrites what the user owns — label, shape, both positions and assignments — and a unit that stops being reported is marked absent rather than deleted, because unplugging an Elegoo's Canvas hub must not discard what was in it. The agreed vocabulary and shape: `docs/printer-storage-terms.md` — `renderer/inventory.js`, `renderer/printers/*/index.js`, `docs/printer-storage-terms.md`, `docs/firestore-schema.md`, `llms.txt`
- Groundwork for assigning an inventory spool to a machine slot: one resolver (`renderer/printers/filament.js`) turns a TigerTag spool into the values any of the six brands can be told — Bambu's catalogue id and tray type, Creality's id and pressure, the family/fill split, the vendor token, the temperatures, and each brand's own colour format. Written from the mobile app's proven behaviour, not from its code. Never refuses: an unknown brand or material becomes a Generic stand-in in the right colour at the right temperature, and the result flags whether the identity is exact. Internal — no user-facing change yet — `renderer/printers/filament.js`
- A connected machine's feed slots are their own movable object on the printer board, next to the machine rather than stuck under its card — placed, snapped and selected like anything else there. The board itself now keys everything off one `data-board-id` instead of assuming one object per machine, which is what the AMS racks will plug into. The widget shows the same coloured squares the machine's own panel draws, each carrying its material family (PLA, ABS, PETG…), at exactly a rack slot's size and scale in picture view (64 × 64, 6 px apart) — a machine's bay and a shelf's bay hold the same object, so a spool must not appear to change size when it moves between them — in balanced rows of at most five, the widget sizing itself to its bays. Four states, the same four the machine's own panel draws: an unknown bay reads `?`; a bay told what it holds but **not loaded** shows its colour as a ring with a hollow `?` centre, so it can never be mistaken for a full one; a colour with no declared material reads `—`; a loaded bay is filled with its material. Hovering one names the bay, the filament's brand and its variant (*E1 — R3D · PLA Basic*); an empty bay is drawn as an outline rather than skipped, so the strip never shortens when a spool runs out. Each brand now normalises its own wire format — AMS trays, CFS materials, ACE slots, tool-changer nozzles — behind one `getSlots` on the brand registry, so the slots can be read anywhere instead of only inside each brand's own filament card — `renderer/inventory.js`, `renderer/printers/*/index.js`, `renderer/css/40-printers.css`
- A move handle on every card, left of the ⋮, on both boards. Outside Arrange it is the only thing that moves a machine or a rack — the rest of a printer card opens its panel, and a rack no longer slides because you reached past its title — while in Arrange the whole card stays a handle. New `icon_move` glyph — `renderer/inventory.js`, `renderer/css/30-racks.css`, `renderer/css/40-printers.css`, `renderer/css/70-detail-misc.css`, `assets/svg/icons/icon_move.svg`
- Rubber-band selection on both boards — sweep the empty board to select several machines (Printers) or racks (Storage, in Arrange mode only), shift to add to the selection, then drag any selected card to move the whole group at its existing offsets. One shared implementation (`wirePlanMarquee`) so the two boards cannot drift apart — `renderer/inventory.js`, `renderer/css/30-racks.css`, `renderer/css/40-printers.css`

## Changed
- The printer board's Arrange button and zoom float over the board's top-right corner instead of sitting on a strip above it: that strip cost a row of height on every screen for two controls used occasionally, and the corner is where the eye already goes for a map's controls. Anchored to the view rather than to the board, so panning sideways does not carry them off — `renderer/inventory.js`, `renderer/css/40-printers.css`
- Everything on the printer board shares one width, so a machine card and the unit beside it line up. It is derived rather than chosen — exactly what a four-slot unit measures, padding and border included — so changing a slot's size carries it along. A unit with more slots is allowed to be wider rather than squeezing its squares — `renderer/css/40-printers.css`
- The board's group/ungroup menu opens on a right-click **anywhere**, including inside a group's own ring — the outline is drawn without pointer events, so it never swallows a click meant for the board beneath it and is hit-tested by geometry instead. Right-clicking inside a ring is the most natural way to ask about that group, and it was the one place the menu did not answer. It also opens over bare board: after sweeping a rubber band the natural next gesture is a right-click where you finished, not a trip back onto one of the cards. Off a card it acts on the current selection — `renderer/inventory.js`
- A printer card is a switch: clicking the machine whose panel is already open closes it, instead of leaving the ✕ as the only way back — `renderer/inventory.js`
- Panning both boards is now the wheel button only; the left button on empty board draws a selection instead — `renderer/inventory.js`
- Several racks can be selected at once, where it used to be one (Arrange mode, as before — outside it a press on the board still belongs to the racks and their slots) — `renderer/inventory.js`, `renderer/css/30-racks.css`
- In a group move, each carried card answers for its own landing spot, so the red overlap edge names the machine or rack actually sitting on something. Still a warning — a drop is never refused — `renderer/inventory.js`
- **The printer view is a board now, like Storage.** It used to split into "Connected" and "Offline" sections, so a machine losing its link changed place — you lost track of it at the moment you were looking for it, and a card you had put somewhere never stayed put. One flat list: the state is already a coloured dot on the card and did not also need to be a change of address. Each machine is then placed by hand, on a permanent grid, with the storage plan's own magnetism and guides — the geometry and the panning were made board-agnostic rather than duplicated, so one implementation serves both. Wheel to zoom, middle-drag to pan, buttons and a typed value from 30 % up, and the point under the cursor is held still so zooming can never be mistaken for something having moved. The board carries its own zoom (`tigertag.printer.planZoom`), since the two plans hold different things at different scales. The position lives on the printer as `plan: {x, y, z}`, so it follows the account; a machine never placed flows below the arrangement instead of landing on top of it, and the one moved last sits in front. **The whole of the old organisation went with it**: the sort drag-and-drop that rewrote `sortIndex` behind the arrangement, the state partition and its section headers, the full grid rebuild that fired whenever a machine blinked — a connect or a disconnect now touches one dot, because nothing about a card's position depends on its state any more — the grip that only ever existed for the sort drag, and the trailing "+ add a printer" card, which had no place on a board and duplicated the "+" in the search bar. The whole card is the handle now: a click still opens the panel, and the move begins after 4 px of travel. `sortIndex` survives as the order of the table view. — `renderer/inventory.js`, `renderer/css/40-printers.css`



## Fixed
- Switching to the printer board could silently do nothing. Its cards and widgets are all assembled before `host.innerHTML` is assigned, so anything throwing in one widget meant the assignment never happened: the view kept whatever it had and looked like it refused to switch, with nothing on screen to say why. A widget that fails is now skipped and logged, and the machines still draw. A deferred rebuild also gets a two-second watchdog — deferring under a hand or an open menu is right, but a deferral that can last for ever is a frozen screen, which is far worse than the flicker it avoids — `renderer/inventory.js`
- Switching view could stop working entirely: the printer board defers its rebuild while a menu is open, and any stray menu parked on `body` — a rack's, or one a previous render failed to sweep — held that deferral for ever. Only a menu belonging to a machine on this board can hold it back now, and changing view closes menus first, since an open menu belongs to the view you are leaving. A guard that can deadlock the screen is worse than the flicker it prevents — `renderer/inventory.js`
- Right-clicking a printer card opened its side panel behind the group menu: the press that arms an open was armed by any button, and a secondary click is a request for a menu, never for a destination — `renderer/inventory.js`
- The temperature widget did not follow its group: the list of a machine's board objects — what cluster membership is built from — knew about its card and its storage but not about it, so a bound set moved without it and left it sitting alone. That list is also where a widget switched off must drop out, which it now does — `renderer/inventory.js`
- Rebuilding the printer view threw the board back to its top-left corner — switching a widget on, or any brand tick, lost wherever you had panned to. The board is a map; redrawing it is no reason to move the camera, so the scroll is restored after the layout that gives the board its size — `renderer/inventory.js`
- Anycubic showed an unreported temperature setpoint as a dash, in both the panel and the board widget. A dash says "no idea", which is honest for a reading — but a heater the machine has given no target for is at zero, and that is what the pill's own editor already assumed when clicked, so the card disagreed with itself — `renderer/printers/anycubic/cards.js`
- Arrange mode no longer opens a machine's side panel. The whole card is a handle there, so every placement ended in a click on it and a panel slid open over the board you were laying out; the ⋮ still answers, so Settings and grouping stay reachable — `renderer/inventory.js`
- Grouping cards nudged them out of place. Grouping writes no position, but it does trigger a layout — and a card that has never been given coordinates is re-placed by every layout pass. Each member is now pinned to where it is drawn before the binding is written, so grouping is visually inert: the outline appears and nothing else moves — `renderer/inventory.js`
- Printer board group moves, audited end to end. A release landing outside the window was heard by nothing, and the cost was not a stuck card but a **frozen board**: anything still held suppresses the rebuild every brand asks for on a live change, so the plan stopped updating until the next drag settled the debt. A group was clamped card by card at the board edge, so dragging one past it flattened the spacing you had arranged — the set is now shifted as one and every gap survives. The cluster outline was destroyed and recreated on each layout pass, blinking twice per group move, and is patched in place instead; it also re-reads its origin if a zoom re-lays the board mid-drag — `renderer/inventory.js`
- Moving a group of cards on the printer board flashed them back to their old position before landing. Five causes, all about ORDER: letting go and immediately grabbing again overlapped two gestures, and the first one finishing unlocked the board while the second was still running — holds are counted now, so the board is free only when the last is done; an object in the hand answers for its own position and the record is not consulted for it at all; cards were released from "held" when the button came up rather than when the writes landed, leaving a window in which any brand tick rebuilt the board from stale coordinates; the guard that protects a just-written position was cleared by our OWN optimistic patch, so it evaporated in the tick it was set and the snapshot arriving a moment later won; and, originally, a rebuild deferred during the drag was flushed before the new positions had been written, so it redrew the board from a record that still held the old ones; and a snapshot already in flight rebuilt `state.printers` wholesale, discarding the in-place patch a drop had just made. A long gesture is what makes both likely, which is why a group showed it and a single card almost never did. The deferred rebuild now waits for the writes, and a position just written outranks the record until the record agrees. Original note: a drop patches `state.printers` in place, but the printers subscription rebuilds those objects wholesale from every snapshot — and one already in flight still carried the previous position, so it landed, the patch was gone, and the cards snapped back until the server's echo arrived. A position just written now outranks the record until the record agrees with it — `renderer/inventory.js`
- A right-click on the printer board armed a card grab on its way past, which cleared the very selection the new menu was about to act on. Only the left button moves a card now — `renderer/inventory.js`
- Placing the board no longer sweeps up the marks drawn ON it (the cluster outline, the rubber band) and hands them a spot of their own among the cards — `renderer/inventory.js`
- Saving a machine's position without an explicit stacking order threw: it still called for a machines-only depth counter that went when the board grew one order for everything on it — `renderer/inventory.js`
- A card being dragged on the printer board could teleport away mid-move: every brand rebuilds that whole view whenever a machine's live state changes, which with ten machines reconnecting is constant, and the rebuild detached the very node under the pointer — the hand carried a ghost while a fresh card appeared at the stored position, and the release wrote the ghost's coordinates. Nothing rebuilds or re-places the board while something is held; the rebuild is deferred to the release, not dropped — `renderer/inventory.js`
- A rubber-band sweep that left the board froze its box and left it behind, so the next attempt drew a second one. Leaving the board now abandons the sweep and gives the selection back, with the window catching a release that lands outside it — `renderer/inventory.js`
- Grabbing a card on either board now rings it in the accent colour. The only sign of a grab used to be a drop shadow, which on a dark board under a card that has barely moved is no sign at all — a grab that took and one that missed looked the same. The three states are written as one ordered block per board (held < selected < colliding) so a collision can never be outranked by a lift — `renderer/css/30-racks.css`, `renderer/css/40-printers.css`
- A card dragged past the edge of its board is now dropped where it last stood, on both boards. The card holds pointer capture, so nothing told it the cursor had left — it kept following a pointer that was no longer over the map, and you had to travel back inside just to let go — `renderer/inventory.js`
- Clicking the ⋮ on a printer card also opened the machine's side panel behind the menu: the press armed an open through the rebuild-safety fallback, which the click handler then never got to cancel — `renderer/inventory.js`
- The closed-hand cursor now appears the moment you press a rack or a machine, instead of after the first pixels of travel, and stays closed wherever the pointer goes during the drag — `renderer/inventory.js`, `renderer/css/30-racks.css`
- Overlap warning on the Storage board is a red outline again, and only that: it used to recolour an outline that Arrange mode alone declared (so nothing showed outside Arrange), and the red wash it painted over the card replaced the drag shadow as well — `renderer/css/30-racks.css`
